**Figure 3 | Recoverability is heterogeneous across targets, experimental environments and phenotype content.** **a,** Rank spectra of KO-level Pearson correlation for reporters, physical screens and reporter–screen assays under field, held-out-gene and strict whole-screen evaluation. Points show the unweighted median across ten frozen predictors; vertical intervals show the predictor Q25–Q75 range. Thin categorical strips give biological-system membership in the same left-to-right order as each rank spectrum; physical-screen tiles are subdivided when a screen contains reporters from multiple systems. Coloured count strips report the numbers of low (r < 0.60), intermediate (0.60–0.75) and high (r ≥ 0.75) units. Rings identify three frozen, score-independent microscopy examples shown at right; images are descriptive and do not enter the statistics. **b,** Held-out-gene recoverability grouped by biological system (reporter as the statistical unit) and endpoint family (reporter–family as the statistical unit). Pale points show individual units, large points show means and horizontal lines show the frozen 95% intervals. **c,** Marker information value and undirected phase–marker overlap for 52 reporters, with colour denoting directed held-out-gene recoverability. The aligned forest and numerical rail report Spearman associations between directed recovery and full-depth marker value, marker value at a matched observation depth, or phase–marker overlap; intervals are reporter-bootstrap 95% confidence intervals. The weak association with phase–marker overlap demonstrates that observing related biological structure is not equivalent to reconstructing a targeted measurement.
